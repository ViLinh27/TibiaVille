# <u>**PRISON RIGHTS**</u>
---
## Cornell law school
### Links
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/prisoners%27_rights
### Notes
- prisoners do not have full constitutional rights
  - protected by 8th amendment's prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment.
  - afforded minimum standard of living
- there was a case where overpopulation  violated the 8th amendment in California
#### Classification
- where are they going after sentencing? Max security ? min security? solitary?
- courts don't like limiting what states can do to classify prisoners
- federal prisons have full discretion to control prisoner classification
  - federal bureau of prisons has the say

#### Americans with Disabilities Act
- a case in 1998 helped make it mandated legally that prisons have to provide reasonable accommodation for inmates with disabilities

#### Prison Litigation
- basically courts said you have to exhaust all possibilities in legal system before challenging some condition of your confinement in court ( to my understanding)

#### Judicial Review
- the courts are not strict about how prison staff are with prisoner rights so they leave it up to the prison staff. So long as conditions don't infringe on constitutional rights and are within the sentence, courts don't have strict scrutiny.

## ACLU on prisoners rights
### Links
https://www.aclu.org/know-your-rights/prisoners-rights
### Notes
- From ACLU's Prisoner's Rights issue
- Last updated : Sep 3 2025
- Covers various situations of potential abuse suffered in the prison system and explains what to do to protect yourself as an inmate since prison officials legally have to protect you under the 8th amendment of the constitution
  - assault / excessive force
    - 8th amendment violated (maybe) if prison staff knew about assault risk by other prisoners but failed to respond 
  - religious discrimination
  - gender discrimnation (trans usually)
  - pregnancy
  - disabled in prison
  - wanting to receive publications in the mail
  - wanting to send / receive mail in prison
